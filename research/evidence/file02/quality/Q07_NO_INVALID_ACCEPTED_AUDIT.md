For every run, direct replay from frozen 242-account genesis applied only valid included bytes at their observed block heights. The replayed full state had **482 initial and 482 final supply units**, 242 accounts, and an AppHash matching the live oracle. Comet block header at height H carries application state hash from H−1; replay compares at H−1 and requires every observed valid commit before H. The same source-bound `s07Oracle` implementation (SHA-256 `1cde090e38452986d1718bec81650e21ed5bcf0df0138b9b5b0cc05169c5a859`) checked each validator's committed transaction multiset, same-height BlockID/AppHash, receiver balance/nonce and each used sender balance/nonce before writing `OK`. Thus A13's 84 unused funded sender accounts are covered by the full-state replay and matching AppHash, even though the original live oracle did not query each of them separately.

It produced [`Q07_INITIAL_AUDIT_DIAGNOSTIC.json`](Q07_INITIAL_AUDIT_DIAGNOSTIC.json) with 240 valid and eight A13 runs falsely excluded. That diagnostic is retained, not used for performance inference. A13 actually has 241 funded senders and 84 intentionally unused slots; the corrected, source-committed replay verifies the full 241-sender state without modifying raw or changing the workload. The final output supersedes that diagnostic.

The raw oracle stores an `OK` attestation and common-height hashes, not individual per-validator account snapshots. The combination of live per-account checks for used accounts, identical four-validator BlockID/AppHash, and independent full-genesis replay is the available evidence; it is not a historical dump of every account on every validator. This audit does not establish P2P-origin or independent-host behavior and does not turn the negative RQ1b service endpoint into a DoS conclusion.
